01 The page

Four regions. The chart is the evidence, the banner is the reading, the numbers are the state of play, and the sidebar decides what is being read.

The one rule

Every frame is built from what that bar knew and nothing after it. Scrubbing back to bar 900 shows you bar 900's chart, not today's chart with the right half hidden. That is the whole point: it is the only honest way to ask yourself what you would have read at the time.

02 The chart

Straight lines instead of bars. Each line is a stretch of price that one straight line can speak for; where two meet, the market turned.

What each mark is

Solid linegreen / red / grey
A segment: a stretch of bars one straight line speaks for, within the tolerance. Colour is direction; grey means it went nowhere much.
Black dot
A pivot, where two segments meet. Confirmed — it was knowable at this bar.
Dashed grey leg
The leg in progress. It runs from the newest confirmed turn to where you are standing, and it is the only piece that can still change.
Gold dashed line
The protective level — section 05. Close through it and the trend claim dies.
HH HL LH LLEQH, EQL
What each turn was, against the previous turn of the same kind. EQ means "the same level, within noise".
Pale grey path
The reveal: what happened next. Shown only when the reveal slider is above zero.

03 The vocabulary

Six words carry the whole method. Four of them are about the shape, two about how late you learn it.

swing high
swing low
A turn: price went up into it and down out of it, or the reverse. Not every pivot is one — see the bend, below.
HH / HLhigher high, higher low
This swing high is above the previous swing high; this low above the previous low. Both together is an uptrend, and that is the whole of Dow's definition.
LH / LLlower high, lower low
The mirror. Both together is a downtrend.
EQH / EQLequal high, equal low
Within the noise band of the previous one — a double top rather than a new extreme. Without this band, one tick of noise would make a trend.
push
A leg in the trend's direction. In an uptrend, a low to a high.
pullback
A leg against it. In an uptrend, a high back down to a low.
ATRaverage true range
The size of an average bar right now. Every threshold here is quoted in ATRs rather than ticks, so the same setting means the same thing in a fast session and a dead one.
confirmation lag
Bars between a turn happening and it being trustworthy. It is never zero, and every rule you read inherits it.

A bend is not a turn

04 The six states

What the banner can say, the rule behind it, and the sentence it prints.

Uptrend

The last swing high is higher than the one before, and the last swing low is higher too.

Uptrend: HH 30498.88 at bar 1176 and HL 30434.75 at bar 1170

Downtrend

A lower high and a lower low. The mirror of the above, and nothing more.

Downtrend: LH 30801.12 at bar 1482 and LL 30666.50 at bar 1472

Range

The swings disagree — a higher low with a lower high, or a level that repeated. Price is held between the last major high and low.

Range: EQH and HL disagree, price held between 30667.50 and 31045.00

Weakening

Still a trend, but running out of legs: the newest push is much shorter than the one before it, or the newest pullback gave back most of the push it retraced.

Weakening uptrend: the push into bar 50 ran 0.45 ATR against 1.80 before it

Break

A close beyond the protective swing. The one state that does not wait for a new pivot — it can fire on the bar it happens.

Break: close 30809.00 at bar 1482 above the swing high 30803.88 from bar 1468

Unclear

Fewer than four confirmed swings. An answer, not a failure — early in the data, or after a long straight run, there is genuinely nothing to compare.

Unclear: 3 confirmed swings, 4 needed

Order of precedence

A break outranks everything: if a close has gone through the level, that is the reading, whatever the swings still say. Weakening outranks the plain trend, because a trend you are told is tiring is more useful than one you are told is intact. Range and unclear are what is left.

05 The protective level

The gold line is the one thing that would prove the trend wrong. It is drawn so you can see the break before it happens.

In an uptrend it is the swing low that the last push up started from. The trend is a sequence of higher lows, so a close below that low breaks the sequence, and the claim with it. In a downtrend it is the swing high before the last low.

Two things to read off it. First, it is the tagger's own falsification test, drawn on the chart: the distance from price to that line is how much room the trend still has. Second, it is where a stop would belong if you were trading the trend — a close there means the reason for the trade no longer exists.

A close, not a touch. Price pokes through levels constantly; the close is the market's considered opinion, and the break rule wants the close to clear the level by a margin as well — a tenth of an ATR by default.

06 Reading an event

The sequence to watch for, and the order it arrives in. Press the step button and take one bar at a time.

What you seeWhat it means
The dashed leg
stretches		 Price is extending away from the newest turn. Nothing is confirmed while this is happening — the leg has no end, and the tagger is still reading the structure behind it.
A new black dot
appears		 A turn has been confirmed, several bars after it actually happened. Watch where it lands: if it comes in above the previous low, the uptrend just gained a higher low.
The gold line
jumps		 The protected swing changed — a new push started, so a newer low now defines the trend. The trend just gained room, or gave some up.
Price closes
through the gold line		 The banner turns amber and reads break, naming the bar and the level. This needs no pivot and no waiting; it is the fastest reading on the page.
The banner reads
weakening		 The trend is intact but the pushes are shrinking or the pullbacks deepening. The reason line says which, with the two sizes in ATRs so you can judge the call yourself.
Swing names change
on old dots		 A pivot was withdrawn or a new one appeared behind the edge, and the chain was re-read. Normal near the right edge; if it happens deep in the history, the tolerance is set too fine for these bars.

The drill

Set the reveal to zero, press random, and say out loud what the state is and what you expect next. Then raise the reveal slider and see. The banner tells you what the rules said; the grey path tells you what the market did. Those are two different questions, and getting them confused is the main way people learn the wrong lesson from a chart.

When you disagree

You will, and often you will be right. The tagger has no memory, no context and no view on the session; it reads four swings and a close. A disagreement is a rule worth changing — note the bar number, because that list is the backlog for the next version.

07 The numbers

The row under the chart is the state of play in five figures.

Time
The close time of the bar you are standing on. Worth watching for the session boundary — a straight line fitted across the overnight gap is not a leg.
Turns known
Confirmed pivots in the window. Very few means the tolerance is coarse or the market has been straight; very many means it is fine or the market has been noisy.
Leg in progress
How long the unconfirmed leg has run, and how far in ticks. Compare it with the typical leg: a leg already twice the median is late to be joining.
Newest turnseen N bars late
How long ago the last confirmed turn happened, and how late you learnt it. That second number is the confirmation lag made concrete, bar by bar.
ATR
The average bar, in ticks. Every threshold on the page is a multiple of this.

Below that, What this dataset costs you in lag does the division that matters: how far into the following leg a turn becomes trustworthy. Under a third is room to work with. Over a half means the read arrives after the move, and no rule written on top can give that back — the answer then is a coarser tolerance or a slower timeframe, not a cleverer rule.

08 The knobs

The sidebar splits in two: what gets cut, and what gets judged. Changing the first re-reads the market; changing the second re-reads the cut.

The cut

Tolerancein ATRs
How far a bar may stray from the line drawn over it. The level of detail, and the only knob that really matters. Under 0.3 keeps every swing; 0.75 to 1.5 draws the legs a person would draw; over 3 gives the session in a few strokes.
Method
Bottom up gives the cleanest shapes. Sliding window is rougher and is the only one that could run live — comparing the two is how you find out what that difference costs.
Minimum length
The shortest leg allowed, so a single spike cannot become a leg of its own.
Window
How many bars each re-fit looks back over. Longer is steadier and slower.

The rules

Same level within
The noise band for calling two swings equal. Raise it and marginal higher highs become double tops, and trends become ranges.
Break clears by
How far past the protective level a close must settle. Raise it to ignore pokes; raise it too far and the break arrives after the reversal.
Push shrunk to
Below this fraction of the previous push, the trend is called weakening.
Pullback gave back
Above this fraction of the push it retraced, likewise.
Defaults are a starting point

Every one of these was chosen by argument, not by fitting. They should be argued with against a long export — weeks, not days. A few days of one instrument cannot justify a threshold, and tuning on it is how you end up with rules that describe last Tuesday.

09 Three traps

The right edge argues with itself

The newest leg is provisional and the newest turn can be withdrawn. That is not a bug in the drawing; it is the honest shape of the problem. What settles behind it stays put, and the number that says how long settling takes is the confirmation lag.

The lag is never zero

A turn at bar 1000 that is confirmed at 1003 was invisible for three bars. If the median leg runs six bars, half the move is gone before the turn that started it can be read. Every rule you write inherits that, and it is the first thing to measure on any new timeframe.

The tagger is a ruler, not an oracle

It reads four swings and a close. It has no idea it is ten minutes before the cash open, that the last three days were a range, or that the move it is calling an uptrend is a single bar's news spike. It is consistent, explainable and fast — which is exactly what makes it useful as a training partner and useless as an authority.
